Real or fake.

Counterfeits are common enough that the official USA store posts its own warning about fake websites copying its photos. Work through the six checks below in order. A real figure passes all six. A fake usually fails two or three at once.

First, the website check

The official USA store warns that fake sites use names similar to its own and copy its product images and descriptions without permission. The one official USA and Canada site is sonnyangelusa.com. Before you pay anywhere, read the address bar and compare it letter by letter. The official store also keeps its authorized retailer list on the Store tab of the Sonny Angel USA app, and states that it cannot support products bought from unauthorized sellers.

  1. 1 Packaging

    Real boxes are crisp. Print is sharp, colors match the series art, and the box stands square with clean folds. Fake boxes feel flimsy, with blurry art, off colors, and misspelled words. Check the small print on the back: a real box carries the Dreams copyright line and full product information. A box with thin card, dull print or spelling errors has already told you what is inside.

  2. 2 Print on the figure

    Face print is where fakes fail first. On a real figure the eyes sit level and centered, lashes and brows are fine and even, and the mouth is small and clean. Misaligned eyes, uneven lips, smudged lines or colors that bleed past their edge are counterfeit signs. Hold the figure at arm's length: a real face looks calm and symmetrical.

  3. 3 Paint and finish

    Real paint is matte, even and vibrant, with clean edges between colors and no gloss patches. Fakes show uneven coloring, paint pooled in corners, rough or bubbled surfaces, and visible mold seams. Run a thumb over the headgear: a real figure feels smooth all over, with no sharp flashing along the seam line.

  4. 4 Weight and feel

    A real mini figure has a solid, dense feel for its size, about 3 inches tall. Fakes often feel light and hollow, and some stand wobbly because the body is poorly molded. If you own one known real figure, weigh the suspect against it. A clear weight gap between two figures of the same series is a strong tell.

  5. 5 Markings

    Check for the official markings: the Sonny Angel name, the Dreams copyright information and the series information on the box, printed cleanly and spelled correctly. Missing markings, wrong fonts, or a logo placed off center point to a counterfeit. Retail editions sold through US bookstores also carry a proper product code; Kinokuniya lists Animal Ver. 1 under 4542202653753, for example.

  6. 6 Price

    Know the real numbers first. Standard blind boxes at the official USA store run 12.25 to 13.75 dollars, and HIPPERS are 13.75. Two price patterns mean danger. Far under retail, like a current series figure listed at 4.95, is the classic counterfeit pattern. Far over retail, like a single standard box listed at 73.45, is gouging on a real or fake product alike. Secrets and Robby figures are the honest exception: the Cat Life Robby was reported up to 299 dollars in March 2025.

If you already bought one

Photograph the box and the figure face in daylight before you open anything else. Compare against the official store photo for that series, linked from its row in the archive. If the checks above say fake, contact the seller and your card issuer promptly. The official store asks fraud victims to contact their bank or card issuer immediately.
